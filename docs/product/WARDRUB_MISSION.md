# Wardrub mission — the closet that works for you

## Mission

**Make getting dressed effortless: help people know what they own, where it is,
what is ready to wear, and what works together—so they can get more life and more
joy from the clothes they already have.**

Wardrub's core product is not generated fashion content. It is the work we remove:
remembering clothes, finding them, pairing them, maintaining them, and choosing
what to wear for the actual day ahead.

Our ambition is to become the most trusted clothing-management companion: the
place people consult before opening their cupboard, not another feed to browse.

## Founder's direction

People lose track of large wardrobes. Clothes go into the wash, return, move
between shelves, get overlooked, or stop fitting their routines. Wardrub should
help people capture their wardrobe enjoyably, keep its state useful with very
little effort, and recommend outfits that bring out their own best version.

The desired morning is:

> “I open Wardrub, see an outfit I want to wear, know the pieces are available and
> where to find them, then grab them and get dressed.”

We cannot promise to know where a physical garment is or whether it is clean
without evidence. Earn that trust through easy confirmations, honest uncertainty,
and corrections—not invented tracking.

## Product principles

1. **Management and decisions before generation.** Recommendation quality,
   availability and findability matter more than magazine copy or try-on imagery.
2. **Useful before complete.** Deliver value from a small usable set of clothes;
   do not require users to catalogue the whole closet or finish style analysis.
3. **Make capture rewarding.** Batch capture, suggested attributes, quick fixes,
   immediate outfit possibilities and gradual additions. Avoid turning setup into
   a survey, an AI wait, or a completion-score chore.
4. **Low-effort maintenance.** Batch laundry actions, one-tap wear confirmation,
   optional shelf/drawer labels, clear undo, and no repeated information requests.
5. **Personal style belongs to the wearer.** Learn their preferences, offer gentle
   exploration, and keep every default correctable. Color seasons and fashion
   conventions are suggestions, not rules about what a person may wear.
6. **Ground recommendations in reality.** Use actual wardrobe items, current
   availability, relevant weather and optional occasion/context. Say when unknown.
7. **Help existing clothes earn their place.** Support rotation, rediscovery and
   care; do not optimize primarily for buying more clothes or app engagement.
8. **Respect privacy and costs.** No biometric enrollment is needed for the core
   recommender. Reuse approved data and computation; do not regenerate editorial
   content just because another day has passed.

## Success means

- Less time and uncertainty choosing an outfit.
- More recommendations people actually choose and wear.
- Fewer “that is in the wash / I cannot find it” surprises.
- More useful wear from the existing wardrobe, with less tracking effort.
- Trust that Wardrub understands preferences without demanding constant input.

Longer clothing life is an ambition to evaluate over time, not an impact claim
we can infer from clicks or wear counts alone.
